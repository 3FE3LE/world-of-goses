# World of Goses — Lineages and Professional Affinities

> Canonical design pillar for citizens, professional learning, and the founding hero.
> The eight lineage names are working terms until they pass the project's originality review.

## 1. Core principle

Every citizen can learn every profession and competency.

Lineage may influence:

- Initial learning speed.
- Ease of understanding particular techniques.
- Experience retention after a period without practice.
- Error likelihood while learning.
- Ability to transmit a skill to an apprentice.
- Adaptation to particular tools or conditions.
- Early appearance of related institutions.
- Initial cultural prestige of certain work.

Lineage must not:

- Block a profession.
- Establish a permanent level ceiling.
- Guarantee competence.
- Replace real experience.
- Make all members share the same interests.
- Turn an affinity into an automatic production bonus.

A citizen without a common lineage affinity can completely surpass a citizen who has one through individual aptitude, experience, education, mentorship, tools, health, motivation, opportunity, and high-quality institutions.

## 2. Five layers of competence

Professional development is the interaction of five layers:

```text
lineage
+ personal aptitudes
+ available education
+ practical experience
+ current condition
```

### Lineage

A partially shared bodily, cognitive, environmental, and cultural predisposition. It is strongest at the beginning and must lose relative weight as experience and opportunity accumulate.

### Personal aptitudes

Individual variation that can agree with or contradict the tendencies of the citizen's lineage. Aptitude is not destiny and is not a production multiplier.

### Education

Mentors, schools, documentation, institutions, and access to teaching.

### Experience

Work actually performed, problems solved, and events survived. Experience is the main source of final competence.

### Current condition

Health, tools, rest, environment, motivation, safety, and other circumstances that can change from day to day.

## 3. Professional families

A concrete profession may belong to more than one family.

1. **Extraction:** mining, quarrying, logging, gathering, excavation, prospecting.
2. **Construction and infrastructure:** masonry, structural carpentry, roads, bridges, hydraulics, fortification, maintenance.
3. **Agriculture and living systems:** cultivation, livestock, forestry, breeding, soil management, water treatment.
4. **Medicine and care:** first aid, surgery, pharmacology, rehabilitation, mental health, community care.
5. **Engineering and manufacturing:** mechanics, smithing, automation, industrial alchemy, demolition, fabrication, repair.
6. **Exploration and survival:** cartography, navigation, tracking, hunting, camps, reconnaissance, survival.
7. **Logistics:** transport, storage, inventories, distribution, expedition preparation, supplies.
8. **Commerce and administration:** accounting, negotiation, valuation, insurance, public management, contracts, economic planning.
9. **Research and education:** research, diagnosis, statistics, history, astronomy, teaching, institutional design.
10. **Social relations:** diplomacy, mediation, psychology, performance, translation, intelligence, communication.
11. **Security and combat:** defence, vigilance, tactics, escort, combat, rescue, threat handling.
12. **Arts and culture:** music, narrative, design, ceremonies, visual arts, cultural preservation.

The family list is an organising vocabulary, not a class system.

## 4. The eight lineages

The following descriptions are qualitative catalog data. They describe common entry points and contextual frictions, not exclusive careers.

### Ardhen

Marked affinities: extraction; construction and infrastructure; medicine and care; logistics.

Moderate affinities: engineering and manufacturing; security and combat; agriculture and living systems.

Ardhen often learn body-aware techniques first: efficient posture, weight distribution, exertion rhythm, vibration reading, safe tool use, structural repair, trauma first aid, casualty transport, rehabilitation, and physical logistics.

Possible cultural frictions include abstract work without visible application, administrative or domestic work that receives little recognition, emotional health, and prolonged diplomacy without operational feedback. These frictions are contextual and can be removed by education and institutional recognition.

### Eirune

Marked affinities: agriculture and living systems; medicine and care.

Moderate affinities: research and education; exploration and survival; engineering and manufacturing.

Eirune often learn by perceiving relationships between organisms, cycles, water, soil, nutrition, disease, breeding, fermentation, toxicology, biodiversity, and environmental recovery.

Possible frictions include extraction without restoration, mechanised work that removes direct observation, and decisions that prioritise immediate efficiency over biological cycles. An Eirune can still become an excellent miner through mineral biology, toxicology, ventilation, cave knowledge, and land recovery.

### Kovari

Marked affinities: engineering and manufacturing; extraction; logistics.

Moderate affinities: construction and infrastructure; security and combat; medicine and care.

Kovari often learn through components, diagnosis, modular design, repair, disassembly, tool adaptation, automation, explosives, technical excavation, material recovery, workshops, and component supply chains.

Possible frictions include opaque institutions, systems designed not to be repaired, techniques transmitted only by tradition, and rigid plans that forbid intervention. A Kovari can enter medicine through prostheses, instruments, technical diagnosis, or clinical automation.

### Myrven

Marked affinities: social relations; commerce and administration; medicine and care.

Moderate affinities: arts and culture; security and combat; research and education.

Myrven often learn through roles, behaviour, perspective, language, privacy, negotiation, mediation, public communication, investigation, informants, psychology, trauma support, law, and public process design.

Possible frictions include institutions without privacy, inflexible roles, work that values only visible physical output, and repetition stripped of social context. A Myrven can be a capable miner as a crew leader, labour mediator, theft investigator, or safety coordinator.

### Vaelun

Marked affinities: exploration and survival; logistics; extraction.

Moderate affinities: social relations; commerce and administration; construction and infrastructure.

Vaelun often learn through movement, terrain, weather, routes, navigation, cartography, tracking, water finding, resource recognition, convoy preparation, territorial distribution, first contact, and frontier diplomacy.

Possible frictions include extremely sedentary work, institutions tied to a single location, and plans without alternate routes. A Vaelun can be an excellent farmer by adapting seeds, moving crops, and recognising suitable terrain.

### Orveth

Marked affinities: commerce and administration; logistics.

Moderate affinities: social relations; agriculture and living systems; medicine and care.

Orveth often learn through exchange, valuation, contracts, credit, insurance, inventory, storage, reserves, accounting, scarcity forecasting, transport cost, route profitability, hospitality, markets, and services for visitors or migrants.

Possible frictions include work whose value is difficult to record, informal distribution, projects with uncertain returns, and institutions that ignore reserves and risk. An Orveth city can produce excellent doctors by building distribution, insurance, supply reserves, and hospital administration.

### Caelith

Marked affinities: research and education; medicine and care; commerce and administration.

Moderate affinities: engineering and manufacturing; exploration and survival; social relations.

Caelith often learn through diagnosis, statistics, experiment, history, astronomy, geology, biology, economic analysis, documentation, models, curricula, prediction, policy, institutional design, and risk analysis.

Possible frictions include urgent work without time for analysis, knowledge transmitted without explanation, institutions that reject revision, and work that depends mostly on improvised bodily response. A Caelith can become a miner through geology, structural analysis, vein planning, and predictive safety.

### Theryn

Marked affinities: medicine and care; social relations; arts and culture; security and combat.

Moderate affinities: research and education; commerce and administration; logistics.

Theryn often learn through shared practice, emotional communication, group organisation, motivation, crisis response, team formation, ceremonies, music, narrative, memory preservation, evacuation, rescue, and coordinated defence.

Possible frictions include isolated work, institutions that suppress expression, purely technical work without human contact, and systems that value results while ignoring group wellbeing. A Theryn can excel in mining through crew coordination, panic prevention, collapse response, and worker care.

## 5. One profession, many paths

Mining is not an Ardhen-only profession.

- **Ardhen:** body, tools, load, rhythm, and practical safety.
- **Eirune:** mineral biology, toxicity, environmental impact, and restoration.
- **Kovari:** machinery, explosives, automation, and recovery of components.
- **Myrven:** labour management, social safety, internal investigation, and mediation.
- **Vaelun:** prospecting, routes, terrain, and resource location.
- **Orveth:** concessions, costs, storage, contracts, and distribution.
- **Caelith:** geology, prediction, vein planning, and structural safety.
- **Theryn:** coordination, morale, emergency response, and care.

All can become great miners, but not necessarily through the same route.

## 6. Citizen profile and the end of resonance

The onboarding profile does not generate a primary lineage plus secondary resonances. It stores:

```text
lineage
personal aptitudes
professional affinities
elemental affinity
combat style and weapon preferences
personality traits
political orientation
spiritual posture
```

The current implementation stores these choices as immutable, validated profile metadata. It displays them in onboarding and in the hero profile, but does not yet turn them into production percentages or skill ceilings.

A person may be Ardhen while choosing medicine, research, construction, water, polearms, patience, a communitarian orientation, and a contemplative spiritual posture. Another person may be Eirune while choosing extraction, exploration, demolition, fire, heavy weapons, risk tolerance, and a security-oriented political posture. Neither person stops belonging to their culture because their path is less common.

The profile is not a replacement for professional experience. Future skill mechanics must make the weight of real work, education, tools, health, motivation, and opportunity increase over time.

## 7. Data and balance rules

A lineage definition may eventually expose qualitative dimensions such as learning affinity, retention affinity, teaching affinity, environmental adaptation, and cultural availability. Those dimensions are not profession assignments stored on the lineage itself.

A citizen stores their lineage and individual profile separately from competencies, roles, professional history, education, health, assignments, and events. A citizen can change profession without changing lineage. A hero is an ordinary citizen carrying the `Hero` recognition.

The first mechanical effects should be small and causal. They may influence early learning, error context, fatigue, retention, or transfer between related skills. They should not make lineage the greatest determinant of final performance. Do not introduce values such as `Mining +15%` or `Medicine -5%` before the skill system and its experiments exist.

The city’s environmental alignment remains a separate emergent system. It comes from accumulated policies and actions, not from selecting a permanent faction or elemental affinity during character creation.

## 8. Founding hero and current slice

A new world starts with no citizens and no buildings. The player completes onboarding to establish exactly one hero. The hero profile is saved in schema v2. Existing prototype saves containing the old five citizens and three buildings are not migrated into the new story; they are left untouched until a successful onboarding save replaces them atomically.

Buildings remain valid domain concepts and will return through a future authorised construction decision. The absence of buildings is therefore a legitimate early-world state, not a broken save. The next production slice should make the first building a consequential player decision based on the hero, available knowledge, materials, and authorisation.

The eight lineages and profile choices are foundation data. They are not a promise that every future system must be implemented at once. Every new mechanic must still demonstrate a real decision, a causal consequence, a visible explanation, persistence, and the same live/offline rule.
